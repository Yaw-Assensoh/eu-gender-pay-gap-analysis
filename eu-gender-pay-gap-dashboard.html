Where the EU gender pay gap is highest, and how it varies

An interactive comparison of reported gender pay gaps across EU countries, economic sectors and age groups. A higher value means a larger gap between men's and women's average gross hourly earnings, in the direction of women earning less; a negative value, where shown, indicates the reverse. Use the year slider to explore the available observations.

Methodology, definitions and limitations
  • Country mean: arithmetic mean of the non-missing country values for the selected year. Each reporting country contributes equally, regardless of population or employment size.
  • Time series: the same calculation is repeated independently for each year. The set of countries with available observations can change from year to year.
  • Sector mean: arithmetic mean of the non-missing sector values displayed for the selected year. This is a descriptive reference line and is not the country mean.
  • Age groups: the age chart displays the supplied series as embedded in this project. No additional weighting or causal analysis is applied in this page.
  • Missing values: null observations are excluded from calculations and shown as unavailable where applicable. They are not treated as zero.
  • Interpretation: the page is descriptive. It does not establish that age, sector or geography causes a particular pay-gap value.
  • Data validation: the values embedded in the original project have been retained. Before publication, verify the extraction date, Eurostat filters, units, and revisions against the source datasets.

The unadjusted gender pay gap is a descriptive indicator and should not be interpreted as a direct estimate of unequal pay for the same work.

Data sources: Eurostat datasets sdg_05_20, earn_gr_gpgr2 and earn_gr_gpgr2ag, as cited in the original project. The original file states that the data were retrieved in 2025; the embedded values have not been independently refreshed in this revision.
Calculation: unweighted arithmetic means of non-missing values. Country, sector and age-group series are kept separate.
